Movement gizmos

Select an object (click it, or use the Objects list). In the world a gizmo appears: drag the X, Y or Z arrow to slide along that axis, or drag the centre cube to move freely on the camera plane. Movement is unlimited — there are no slider clamps. Hold Shift while dragging to snap to a 0.25 unit grid.

How this renderer works

Every pixel fires rays from the camera into an analytic scene of boxes, spheres, a glossy floor and camera bodies. Rays scatter through 1–8 bounces for diffuse, metal, glass, glossy and emissive materials. Sun and neon samples are importance-sampled, and volumetrics integrate sun shafts with Henyey–Greenstein scattering. Each frame adds one HDR sample into a float buffer, so the image cleans up asymptotically while you hold still.

Performance notes

  • Object data travels in a float texture instead of hundreds of uniforms — more objects, fewer driver limits.
  • Volumetrics trace only on primary rays; indirect bounces skip fog.
  • Adaptive resolution targets a 60 fps budget, with auto-denoise at low sample counts.
  • Final renders are tiled, so 4K/8K work at any GPU texture limit.
  • Use the GTX 1070 preset for the intended 1080p/0.75-scale experience.

About Ray Tracing Demo 4

Ray Tracing Demo 4 is the fourth recode of a browser ray tracer that started as a “Ray Tracing vs Rasterization” essay and a proof of concept. It is still software ray tracing: no RT cores, no WebGPU, no WebAssembly, no native code — just JavaScript, CSS, HTML and a WebGL2 fragment shader.

Demo 3 introduced volumetric lighting, a render camera with picture-in-picture, selection outlines and a 100k-sample export pipeline. Demo 4 keeps every one of those features and rebuilds around them: real movement gizmos, importance-sampled neon and sun lighting, glossy floors, depth of field, bloom, denoise, seven scene presets, scene save/load, 8K tiled exports, and adaptive performance for cards that have no ray tracing hardware at all.

The planned VR/WebXR mode from Demo 3 is intentionally scrapped and not part of this build.

About ray tracing: ray tracing simulates light by tracing rays from the camera through a scene. At each surface the ray can diffuse, reflect, refract or be absorbed; shadows are simply rays that never reach the sun. Enough samples converge into soft shadows, colour bleeding, caustic-ish glass and fog lit by every emitter in the scene — effects rasterization has to fake.

Original project by Brandon Steele · brandonsteeledev@outlook.com. Kept the classics: “sushi was here”, “Who’s Joe? — J.M.”

Changelog

Demo 4 — “No RT hardware required”

  • Movement gizmos replace position sliders. X/Y/Z arrows plus a free-plane handle live inside the ray traced world; Shift snaps.
  • Lighting rewrite: importance-sampled sun and neon, cosine-weighted diffuse, glossy plastic, metal roughness, adjustable glass IOR.
  • Volumetrics v2: sun shafts with Henyey–Greenstein scattering, height fog, emissive glow, 0–24 quality steps, primary-ray only.
  • World: glossy checker/grid floors, stars at night, animated sun, seven presets.
  • Lens: depth of field with auto-focus, bloom, exposure, temporal denoise, and a final-render HDR denoiser with off/fast/balanced/strong modes.
  • Export: tiled 720p → 8K, PNG/JPG/WebP/BMP, live ETA and cancel; viewport screenshot.
  • Cameras: spawn, relocate, fly, picture-in-picture, RT body meshes.
  • Scenes: JSON save/load, object outliner, remix, selection outlines, camera click-picking.
  • Performance: texture-based object upload, adaptive resolution, quality presets, FPS cap.
  • Removed nothing. VR remains scrapped.
